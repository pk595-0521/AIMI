## Section 1 – Front Matter

## Refined Candidate Instructions

This exercise is designed to simulate a real workplace decision-making process under time pressure. You will receive a role-specific scenario, supporting exhibits, and a live mid-exercise constraint. You may use AI tools, but your process will be evaluated as much as your output.
What we are looking for:

- Clear thinking under uncertainty.
- Strong prioritization.
- Effective use of AI as a work tool.
- Careful verification and data handling.
- Practical judgment that reflects how work gets done in real teams.

For each track, the final recommendation must follow this mandatory structure:

1. Context and recommendation.
2. Top 2 risks.
3. Monitoring / mitigation.
4. Roadmap.
5. Uncertainty acknowledgment.

## Core Evaluation Philosophy

This assessment is designed to measure judgment, not memorization. Candidates should be rewarded for making defensible choices, identifying what matters most, and explaining tradeoffs clearly.
Strong responses will:

- Separate signal from noise.
- Make explicit assumptions.
- Adapt after new information arrives.
- Challenge unsupported AI outputs.
- Handle sensitive data appropriately.
- Produce executive-ready recommendations.

Mid responses will:

- Answer the prompt, but remain generic, incomplete, or only partially adapted to the constraint.

Weak responses will:

- Miss stakeholder context.
- Overfocus on formatting.
- Fail to adapt after the shock.
- Ignore uncertainty.
- Treat AI output as authoritative.

## Universal AI Behavior Rules

Across all tracks:

- At least one AI-generated output will be imperfect, incomplete, or misleading.
- Candidates should verify, challenge, or correct AI output before using it in their final answer.
- In at least one segment, candidates must explicitly decide whether to share, redact, aggregate, or withhold sensitive data before using external AI tools.
- Candidates are not expected to catch everything, but their verification process will be observed and scored.

## Universal Assumption Log

Candidates should record the key assumptions they make before and after the shock event. At minimum, this should include:

- What they think is driving the issue.
- What they believe is uncertain.
- Which assumptions changed after new information arrived.

## Universal AI Verification Log

Candidates should document:

- What AI output they reviewed.
- What was unsupported or incomplete.
- Whether they accepted, modified, or rejected it.
- Why that decision was made.

## Universal Confidence Rating

Candidates should assign a simple confidence rating to their final recommendation, such as:

- High.
- Medium.
- Low.

This rating should reflect the quality of the evidence and the remaining uncertainty.

## Updated Scoring Philosophy

Reviewers should apply the rubric consistently using anchor examples.
Strong response:

- Shows clear prioritization.
- Demonstrates tailored stakeholder awareness.
- Makes explicit tradeoffs.
- Uses verification discipline.
- Handles sensitive data safely.
- Adjusts the plan after the shock.

Mid response:

- Is directionally correct but generic or incomplete.

Weak response:

- Misses the stakeholder context.
- Overindexes on presentation polish.
- Fails to respond meaningfully to the shock.
- Does not acknowledge uncertainty.

## Calibration Guide

Scores should be based on observable actions where possible:

- Number of relevant comparisons or tradeoffs named.
- Whether the candidate explicitly states assumptions.
- Whether the candidate questions AI output.
- Whether the candidate handles sensitive data safely.
- Whether the candidate adapts after the shock.

## Interviewer Notes

- Keep scoring anchored to evidence, not style.
- Do not penalize candidates for choosing a defensible path that differs from your own preference.
- Reward candidates who can explain why they ruled options in or out.
- Use the same standards across all tracks to preserve comparability.

## Section 2 – Consulting Track

## Company Background

Vantage Logistics Solutions is a mid-market B2B supply chain software provider generating $145M in annual revenue. Over the past three years, the company has grown quickly and is now under pressure from the board to improve operating efficiency without expanding headcount.

Leadership believes generative AI can reduce repetitive work across sales, operations, and customer support. Adoption is uneven, however, and leadership is split on whether the company should expand the initiative broadly or keep it limited to a few teams.

The company is preparing for a board update in one month. The COO wants a recommendation that is practical, cost-conscious, measurable, and easy to explain. Sales leadership is politically influential and is concerned that AI could slow representatives down or create customer-facing compliance issues.

The candidate is acting as a consulting associate supporting the COO. The expected output is not simply an answer to a case question: it is a short executive presentation supported by quantitative analysis, explicit assumptions, and a recommendation that can survive stakeholder pushback.

## Structured Workflow

The consulting case follows a professional case-team progression:

**Frame the problem → analyze the evidence → synthesize the story → build the recommendation → respond to a live operating shock → present the final answer**

The candidate should not attempt to analyze every available fact. The strongest candidates identify the few drivers that matter most, quantify the decision where possible, and build a coherent story from evidence to recommendation.

## Scoring Anchor

**Strong response**

- Frames the decision around measurable economic value rather than adoption alone.
- Segments the problem by function and risk profile.
- Performs the key ROI calculations correctly.
- Distinguishes reported productivity from realized savings.
- Recognizes that sales has a different compliance and customer-risk profile.
- Builds a coherent executive storyline rather than a collection of observations.
- Changes the recommendation after the production incident without overreacting to a single data point.
- Uses AI for leverage while independently verifying its claims.

**Weak response**

- Treats adoption as the objective.
- Uses reported hours saved as guaranteed savings.
- Recommends a company-wide mandate without addressing sales risk.
- Copies the AI conclusion.
- Produces slides with lots of data but no clear answer.
- Treats the incident as either irrelevant or automatically fatal without assessing severity and dependencies.

## What Success Looks Like

The final consulting answer should answer five questions:

1. **What is happening?**
2. **What is driving it?**
3. **Where is the economic opportunity?**
4. **What should Vantage do now?**
5. **What changed after the production incident?**

The recommendation should be specific enough that the COO could take it into a board meeting without having to translate a generic case answer into an operating plan.

## Section 3 – Investment Banking Track

## Company Background

Aegis Cyber-Infrastructure, a founder-led mid-market technology security firm, is evaluating the acquisition of Vigil Networks, a smaller niche competitor, to accelerate its product roadmap and expand market share. Aegis has strong product architecture overlap with Vigil, but the transaction is taking place in a volatile market where debt capital is expensive, equity investors are cautious, and management needs to demonstrate strict capital discipline.

The Aegis CEO is growth-oriented but risk-sensitive regarding balance sheet exposure. Your senior Managing Director has emphasized that Aegis is a critical long-term relationship for the investment bank. The client wants a thoughtful strategic recommendation, not a boilerplate spreadsheet model, and needs to know what operating assumptions matter most before moving to exclusivity.

## Structured Workflow

The candidate must move through the same sequence an investment banking team would use on a live transaction:

> **Understand the deal → Build the valuation → Defend the valuation → Receive new information → Re-underwrite the deal → Advise the client**

The exercise is intentionally designed so that the candidate cannot succeed by producing an attractive valuation alone. They must connect the strategic rationale to operating assumptions, build a defensible valuation, explain the model, respond to a material legal shock, and translate the analysis into a senior-client recommendation.

## Exhibits

**Exhibit 1: Deal Snapshot**

- Target Proposed Purchase Price: **$180M**.
- Target LTM Revenue: **$42M**.
- Target LTM EBITDA Margin: **18%**.
- Revenue Growth: **22% YoY**.
- LTM EBITDA: candidate-calculated from revenue and margin.
- Peer Transaction Comparables: four anonymized sector transactions with EV/EBITDA multiples ranging from **18.0x to 23.0x**.
- Strategic rationale: Aegis expects product architecture overlap to accelerate product development and increase market share, but management has not yet proven the size or timing of integration benefits.

**Exhibit 2: Stakeholder Notes**

- **CEO:** Wants top-line growth but does not want to absorb hidden legal liabilities, excessive leverage, or catastrophic integration friction.
- **Senior Banker:** “This deal is highly strategic for our long-term advisory relationship with Aegis. Provide a rigorous, independent view, but give them a viable path forward.”
- **Management:** Expects a clear directional recommendation before entering exclusivity.
- **CFO:** Wants the acquisition to preserve capital flexibility and does not want the transaction case to depend on aggressive synergy assumptions.

**Exhibit 3: AI Output Sample**

An AI-generated deal evaluation memo concludes:

> “Vigil Networks is likely worth more than the $180M asking price based on its outstanding 22% revenue growth rate alone. Because product architecture overlap is high, post-acquisition systems integration will be straightforward. The candidate must identify which statements are supported by the exhibits, which are assumptions, and which require additional diligence.

## Scoring Anchor

**Strong response**

Links the strategic rationale to valuation, constructs the DCF correctly, triangulates rather than blindly selecting one valuation method, identifies the assumptions driving value, explains the model clearly, and materially re-underwrites the deal after the legal shock. Strong candidates recognize that legal uncertainty can affect both expected cash flows and the appropriate level of confidence in the valuation.

**Weak response**

Treats valuation as a mechanical exercise, chooses whichever methodology produces the most attractive number, relies on revenue growth alone, ignores cash-flow conversion, accepts AI output without verification, or simply subtracts $15M from the valuation without considering continuing litigation and transaction structure.

## What Success Looks Like

A strong candidate should be able to:

- articulate a concise investment thesis;
- identify the few operating assumptions that actually drive the deal case;
- build a simplified five-year DCF from raw inputs;
- use transaction comps as a valuation cross-check;
- explain why the DCF and comps may deserve different weights;
- identify downside cases without pretending to know more than the data supports;
- verbally walk a senior banker through the DCF;
- re-underwrite the deal after the legal shock;
- recommend an appropriate transaction posture and next step; and
- communicate the final recommendation in a CEO-ready format.

### 2C — Valuation Judgment (5 min)

> **Which valuation method deserves more weight in this situation—DCF, transaction comps, or a blended view—and why?**

They should explicitly discuss:

- quality of the forecast assumptions;
- market volatility;
- transaction comparability;
- strategic value;
- and the degree of uncertainty surrounding the target.

The candidate is **not** allowed to simply select one method and ignore the others. Both DCF and transaction comps must be considered before deciding how to triangulate the range.

### 2D — Verbal “Walk Me Through a DCF” (5 min)

The candidate receives a technical interview prompt:

> **“Walk me through how you would build a DCF from beginning to end.”**

A strong answer should cover, at minimum:

1. Project free cash flow over approximately 5–10 years.
2. Determine the appropriate WACC.
3. Discount projected FCFs to present value.
4. Calculate Terminal Value using either a perpetuity-growth or exit-multiple approach.
5. Discount Terminal Value to present value.
6. Sum PV of forecast FCFs and PV of Terminal Value to arrive at Enterprise Value.
7. Bridge from Enterprise Value to Equity Value when relevant by accounting for net debt and other applicable adjustments.

The candidate should explain the logic rather than recite terminology.
